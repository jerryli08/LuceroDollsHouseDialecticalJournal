Dialectical Journal — A Doll's House

Text: A Doll's House by Henrik Ibsen

Jerry Li
Ms. Lucero Period 4B
Passage Pages Response
1. "Is that my little lark twittering out there?" 1-9 (R, E) The nickname "little lark" makes it feel like Torvald is treating Nora like a pet or someone less important than him. The bird imagery shows how Nora is kind of trapped and just putting on an act in her life.
2. "Still I think the sick are those who most need taking care of." 10-19 (R, C) Mrs. Linde says this and it's kind of ironic because Nora sacrificed everything to take care of Torvald when he was sick, but he has no idea. This quote makes me think about how women are expected to be caregivers but don't get any credit for it. Mrs. Linde understands this sacrifice while Torvald could never.
3. "The little ones are begging so hard to be allowed to come in to Mamma." 20-29 (R, E) The nurse tells Nora this and it shows how Nora's being pulled between being a mother and dealing with Krogstad's blackmail. Her children represent the innocent, perfect life she's trying to protect, but she's keeping them away because of all the stress and fear she's feeling. It made me realize how much pressure she's under trying to keep everything separate.
4. "And can tear it into a hundred thousand pieces and burn it up—the nasty dirty paper!" 30-39 (E, R) Nora is talking about the forged IOU and you can feel how desperate she is to just destroy all the evidence and make the problem disappear. Calling it "nasty dirty paper" shows how much she hates what it represents (not just the forgery but how it's trapping her). The violence in "tear" and "burn" shows how intense her emotions are getting.
5. "Have you forgotten that it is I who have the keeping of your reputation? Well, now, I have warned you. Do not do anything foolish." 40-49 (P, R) Krogstad is straight-up blackmailing Nora here and reminding her that he controls whether her secret gets out. The way he talks about "keeping" her reputation makes it sound like he owns it. This power dynamic shows how desperate he is to keep his job, and how he's even willing to ruin her whole life to do it. The threat is very direct and it feels like things can only get worse.
6. "Well, I am like a shipwrecked woman clinging to some wreckage—no one to mourn for, no one to care for." 50-59 (E, R) Mrs. Linde uses this metaphor to describe how alone and lost she feels after losing her husband and having no family. The "shipwrecked" image is really powerful because it shows she's barely surviving and has nothing solid to hold onto. I think it's interesting how different her life is from Nora's on the surface, but they're both kind of trapped in their own ways (Nora by secrets and Mrs. Linde by loneliness and poverty).
7. "So wasn't I entitled to make a merry evening of it after that?" 60-72 (R, E) Dr. Rank is talking about enjoying himself even though he's dying, which is tragic but also makes sense. He knows his time is limited so he wants to have fun while he can. This shows how Rank thinks that since life is short and often unfair, you should at least try to enjoy the good moments. This adds to the theme of appearance versus reality because he's acting cheerful while dealing with something terrible.

Overall Reflection: Throughout A Doll’s House, these moments show how Nora is constantly treated as something fragile or owned by Torvald, by society, and eventually by Krogstad. The pet names, the expectations that women quietly sacrifice, and the pressure to keep up appearances all build on each other to trap her. Characters like Mrs. Linde and Dr. Rank help highlight different kinds of suffering, including loneliness, illness, and quiet endurance, which contrast with Nora’s seemingly “perfect” life. As the play goes on, the stress from secrets, power imbalances, and emotional control keeps building until Nora realizes she has been living in a role instead of making real choices for herself. By the end, it is clear that the problem is not just one lie or one character, but an entire system that treats Nora like a doll rather than a person.
